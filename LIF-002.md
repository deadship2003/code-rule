## LIF-002: Design Alternative Selection and Change Confirmation [Process Standard · development phase | Effective 2026-09-07]

> Origin: 2026-09-07 requirement — when the original requirement is
> flawed, too costly to implement, or architecturally unsound, or when
> an engineering-superior alternative exists, do NOT force the original
> requirement through; present the alternatives for comparison and get
> confirmation before development.

Scope: ALL development work in ALL IT development projects — feature
design, back-end service programs implementing LIF-006, deployment
logic, ops CLIs, install/uninstall flows; feature development under any
LIF-series guideline is equally bound by this entry.

### Goal

Avoid entrenching unsound designs; reduce long-term maintenance cost.

### Trigger Conditions (hitting any one forbids coding the requirement directly)

- The requirement logic has holes or incomplete edge-case coverage;
- A simpler-architecture, more stable, or more maintainable alternative
  exists;
- The design carries performance, security, or compatibility risks;
- Development/maintenance cost is high while business benefit is
  limited.

### Process

1. Lay out the original design plus the alternative(s), with comparison
   information covering at least: implementation approach, pros and cons,
   applicable scenarios, risk notes.
2. Submit to the requirement owner for confirmation; the finally
   confirmed design is the basis for development.
3. If the owner explicitly insists on the original design, implement
   exactly as the user specified even when it is not the engineering
   optimum, and record the potential flaws and the trade-off rationale
   in the documentation.

### Runtime Interaction Constraint

- When an ops operation hits a multi-path decision point, the program
  must not silently auto-select; it presents the available actions and
  executes only after user confirmation.

### Documentation Constraint

- The finally selected technical design and the trade-off rationale must
  be recorded in design documents or comments, leaving a decision trail.

> Related: LIF-003 (major hazardous operations require prior consent) —
> both embody "confirm first, then execute"; this entry governs design
> selection at development time, LIF-003 governs dangerous commands and
> session-interrupting operations at runtime.
